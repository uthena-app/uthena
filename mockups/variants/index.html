Four design directions for uthena.com

Four pages each: home, category, product. Same content, four different bets. Each pushes past the existing brand guide in a different direction. Pick one, mix two, or keep exploring.

§RECOMMENDATION · 2026-06-13
→ Pick: A · Editorial

Ship the magazine, not the spreadsheet.

Uthena's catalogue is a curated inventory, not a file dump. Every existing course marketplace on the internet (Udemy, Skillshare, AppSumo-style) renders the same way: a 4-up grid of course thumbnails, identical, undifferentiated. The brand guide's v3 baseline (your Variant C) is the safe, correct choice — but it looks like every other SaaS dashboard on the internet.

Variant A — Editorial is the bet that says: this catalogue is a library, not a list. Fraunces serif at 88px. Magazine-grade covers. The exact feeling of Stripe Press or a curated zine. The brand gets a real visual signature on day one.

  • Why A wins: the serif display type is the single highest-leverage design move. It signals "premium" and "curated" without being precious. Every page gets a clear identity.
  • The trade-off: editorial type means slower page reads at scale. The catalog can be slower than a grid if you go full-bleed covers (mitigated by 3-col grid on /browse). Test with 5 users before committing.
  • Best for: positioning Uthena as the "premium PLR operator's tool" rather than a budget content dump. Supports the new one-liner: "the home for digital product entrepreneurs."
  • If you want safer: ship C — Control. It's a strict refinement of your v3 dark mockups. Zero design risk, polished, ships in 2 weeks. Just know it'll look like every other Linear/Resend clone in the space.
  • My honest second pick: D — Signal. It's a louder, more distinctive bet. If Uthena's marketing posture is "stand out" rather than "fit in," D wins. But D requires a confident content team to write the all-caps mono copy well — bad content will make it look gimmicky.
  • What I'd skip: B — Terminal is the most creative of the four but also the most niche. It says "Uthena is for developers" when your buyer is actually digital entrepreneurs. Beautiful for a partner dashboard; wrong for a public marketplace.
